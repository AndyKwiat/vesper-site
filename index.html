How it works

Three quiet steps between a worry and a prayer.

Tell it plainly

Type or speak what is happening: a diagnosis, an exam, a birth, a grudge you want to set down. You do not need religious language. Ordinary words are enough.

Choose the shape

Pick a tradition and a length. Vesper follows the classic form of a prayer: an address, thanks or confession, the request itself, and a closing.

Pray it, keep it

Read it aloud, edit a line, or ask for a gentler version. Save it to your book, set a reminder for the same hour tomorrow, or send it to the person you prayed for.

Traditions

Written in the voice of your own tradition, not a generic one.

Each tradition has its own forms, names for God, and closing words. Vesper learns the conventions so the prayer sounds like it belongs in your home.

Collect · Litany · Intercession

Christian

Catholic, Orthodox, and Protestant voices, with optional scripture echoes from the Psalms and Gospels.

Berakhah · Mi Sheberach · Kavanah

Jewish

Blessings that open with Barukh atah, prayers for healing by Hebrew name, and kavanot to set intention before a mitzvah.

Du'a · Dhikr

Muslim

Personal du'a opening with praise and salawat, closing with Ameen, written in English with the Arabic phrases you already use.

Examples

Real prompts, and the prayers they became.

Gratitude · Universal

Prompt: First night in our new apartment, boxes everywhere, and I am unreasonably happy.

For walls that are ours and a door that locks, for the friends who carried the couch up three flights, for a kitchen we have not yet cooked in: thank you. Let this place become a home slowly, and let everyone who enters it feel welcome.
Healing · Jewish

Prompt: A Mi Sheberach for my grandfather, Yaakov ben Sarah, recovering from a fall.

May the One who blessed our ancestors bless and heal Yaakov ben Sarah. May the Holy One be filled with compassion for him, restore his strength, and grant him a complete healing, a healing of body and a healing of spirit, speedily and soon. And let us say: Amen.
Guidance · Muslim

Prompt: Deciding whether to take a job in another city. I want clarity, not just comfort.

Alhamdulillah for the choice itself. Ya Allah, You know what is good for me and I do not. If this move is good for my faith, my family, and my future, make it easy and bless it. If it is not, turn it away from me and turn me away from it, and make me content with what You choose. Ameen.

Features

Small, careful, and yours.

Your prayer book

Every prayer you keep goes into a private book, ordered by date and by the people you prayed for. Return to a prayer, or see how one has changed over months.

Speak it, hear it

Dictate your prompt hands-free, and have any prayer read back to you in a calm voice, useful at bedside and at bedtime.

Prayer requests from others

Friends can send you a request with a link. You pray, and they receive the prayer with a note that you did.

Private by default

What you write stays on your device unless you choose to share it. Prompts are never used to train anything.

Reminders by the hour

Set reminders on the traditional hours or your own. A gentle notification, never a streak counter.

Edit any line

A prayer is only yours once it sounds like you. Tap a sentence to rewrite it softer, shorter, or in the second person.
